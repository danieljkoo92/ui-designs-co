The part nobody explains

Three ways people find you now

Ten years ago there was one: you ranked on Google or you didn't. Now there are three, and they need different work. This is what the search line on the plans above actually means.

Search engines · SEO

Someone types it into Google

They search "plumber near me" and pick from what comes up — usually the map results, which take about 44% of the clicks.

  • Your business details identical everywhere online — Google reads mismatches as two different businesses
  • A real page for each service, not one page listing everything
  • Pages that load fast on a phone on a weak signal
  • Reviews arriving steadily rather than in one suspicious burst
  • Photos with proper descriptions attached

Direct answers · AEO

Someone gets one answer, not ten links

Google now answers a lot of questions at the top of the page, and phone assistants read one result aloud. Being tenth is the same as being invisible.

  • Your pages answer the actual questions people ask, in their words
  • Question-and-answer sections written to be quotable
  • Hidden labels on your page telling the machine what your hours, area and services are
  • Straight answers on pricing and timing, not "contact us for details"

AI assistants · GEO

Someone asks ChatGPT for a recommendation

People increasingly ask an assistant "who should I call for this in Astoria?" It names a handful of businesses. Being nameable is now its own job.

  • Clear, consistent facts an assistant can repeat without guessing
  • Being described the same way across your site, your profile and directories
  • Content that reads like an answer rather than an advert
  • Your service area and specialities stated in plain sentences, not left implied

Nobody — me included — can promise you a ranking or a number of calls. Google and the AI companies change how this works constantly. What I can promise is that the work above gets done every month and that you see exactly what was done.

Where the money goes

What actually happens each month

A monthly fee should buy visible work. Here's the routine on a Grow or Dominate plan.

Week 1 — Check and fix

Full sweep of the site: broken links, slow pages, forms that stopped sending, anything a browser update broke. Backups verified by actually restoring one.

Week 1 — Listings audit

Your name, address, phone and hours checked across Google, Apple Maps, Bing and the directories that matter. Any mismatch corrected.

Week 2 — Content work

A page improved or added: a service you want more of, a question customers keep asking, a job worth showing off.

Week 2 — Reviews

Requests go out on finished jobs. New reviews get a drafted reply for you to approve. Anything negative comes to you first, before it's answered.

Week 3 — Search & AI signals

The structured labels on your pages checked and extended, question-and-answer content updated, and the phrasing that makes you nameable by assistants reviewed.

Week 3 — Competitors (Dominate)

What the other businesses in your area changed — new pages, new offers, new review volume — and whether it's worth reacting to.

Week 4 — Report

One page: visitors, calls, form fills, what I changed, what I'm doing next month. Plain English, no dashboard homework.

Any time — Your requests

Price change, new hours, holiday closure, a photo from a job this morning. Text me; small changes are same-day.

Straight answers

Do I have to take a plan?

No. The build is a one-time payment and the site is yours either way. Plans exist because a site that's maintained gets found and a site that's abandoned slowly stops working.

Can I cancel?

Any time, before your next billing date. Cancelling stops future billing; it doesn't refund the month you're in. No notice period, no cancellation fee, no phone call you have to sit through.

If I cancel, do I lose my website?

No. The design and code are yours once the build is paid for. What stops is the hosting, the monitoring and the monthly work — you'd need to arrange hosting elsewhere, and I'll hand over what's needed to do that.

Can I start on a plan without a build?

Not currently. The plans are built around sites I've made, so I know exactly what's under the hood. If you've got a site from someone else, text me and I'll tell you honestly whether it's worth working on.

Can I move up or down a tier?

Yes, from the next billing month. Most people start on Starter and move to Grow once they see traffic worth chasing.

What happens if the work doesn't bring in more customers?

I can't guarantee results and I won't pretend otherwise — anyone promising you rankings or a number of leads is guessing. What you get is a monthly report showing exactly what was done, so you can judge whether it's worth continuing. If it isn't, cancel.

Who do I talk to?

Me. There's no support desk and no ticket number. You text the same number you always have.

Not sure which one?

Text me what you do and roughly how busy you are. I'll tell you which plan makes sense — including if the answer is none of them yet.
